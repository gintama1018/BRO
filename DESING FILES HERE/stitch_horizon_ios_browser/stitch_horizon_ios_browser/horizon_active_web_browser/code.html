The Minimalist Pavilion at Lake Como

A masterclass in cantilevered glass, warm Italian limestone, and reflective water courts nestled into the Lombardy cliffs.

Matteo Santoro Milan Bureau · Oct 14, 2024
verified AD Verified

Photography by Hélène Binet / AD Archive

1 of 8

Perched along the precipitous slate rock face near Bellagio, the glass and travertine structure appears less built than exhaled into the natural landscape. Commissioned as an archival sanctum for contemporary sculpture, the pavilion abandons decorative excess in favor of pure panoramic clarity.

format_quote
format_quote
“We did not want an interruption in the horizon. We wanted the lake to step inside, greet the marble floors, and dissolve the threshold.”
— Studio Bellotti & Associati
Bespoke Travertine Colonnade
Archival Viewing Lounge

The roof plane, an 80-millimeter ultra-dense prestressed concrete slab, stretches 14 meters without vertical column supports. Acoustic baffles hidden within the timber soffit suppress reflection, granting the interior the sacred silence of an alpine chapel.
